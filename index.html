Research portfolio · Soft-matter physics & computation

Finding order inside foam.

I tracked individual bubbles in three evaporating soap foams to test a 1952 law of foam coarsening. Along the way I found and fixed the measurement errors that had made the law look wrong.

Key results

Results

Von Neumann’s law holds in sign in all three foams

Question
Does a bubble’s number of neighbours n set how fast it grows, as von Neumann’s law dA/dt = K(n − 6) predicts?
Method
In time-lapses of three foams recorded in Dr. Oh’s lab, I outlined and tracked every bubble, counted its neighbours and fitted K with a median-based estimator. I checked detection against 14 hand-labelled frames and tested prediction models on foams left out of training.
Result
K was positive, with its 95% interval above zero, in all nine foam × time-interval tests: for Foam A, 0.367 px²/s [0.333, 0.400] at Δt = 30 s. The law also predicted bubble growth better than a graph neural network in 8 of 9 held-out tests.

K is not a fixed constant of each foam, though. It rose 1.5× over Foam A’s life, fell 1.5× over Foam C’s, and was negative while Foam F was still wet. On Foam A the detector matched the hand labels closely (F1 0.966; mean neighbour count within 0.03).

What this does not show. Detection accuracy is measured only on Foam A. With no scale bar, K is in px²/s and the three foams are not compared in size. The research summary lists every limitation.

Process · June to October 2026

How the project unfolded

Dates come from the project’s version history. Orange marks the points where the law appeared to fail or a result had to be withdrawn.

  1. June 2026

    Built bubble detection, frame-to-frame tracking and the neighbour graph.

  2. July 10

    First test of the law: K could not be fitted cleanly. A stop rule set in advance paused the neural-network plan.

  3. July 15

    Foam F, imaged every 10 s, gave K = −1.74, as if the law ran backwards (figure).

  4. July 27

    Found that tracking merged bubbles and never split them back; withdrew a tracking-quality headline built on it.

  5. August 3

    Labelled 14 frames by hand. Liquid channels were being counted as bubbles; removing them raised detection F1 from 0.515 to 0.899.

  6. August 6–7

    Audit: withdrew a “neural network beats physics” result trained on rejected data, and traced K = −1.74 to the least-squares estimator.

  7. August 13

    Ran one detector (Cellpose) on all three foams: K positive in all three.

  8. August 22

    All 22 detected neighbour swaps checked by eye: no false positives.

  9. September 2–6

    Matched time intervals in seconds across foams, and found that K changes over each foam’s life.

  10. September 18

    Verification pass: withdrew several claims about edge effects, bubble roundness and junction size.

  11. September 21

    Wrote down a spatial test plan before running the test.

  12. October 9

    Recomputed every reported number from one set of scripts.
